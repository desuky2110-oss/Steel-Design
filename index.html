Engineering Aspect 🇪🇬 ECP 205 (Egypt ASD / LRFD) 🇪🇺 Eurocode 3 (EN 1993-1-1 / 1-8) 🇺🇸 AISC 360-22 (LRFD / ASD) Engineering Verdict & Efficiency
Safety Calibration Safety factors: Ωy=1.67, Ωu=2.0, Ωc=1.67–1.92. Unfactored service loads. Partial factors: γM0=1.00, γM1=1.00, γM2=1.25. Factored loads (1.35G + 1.5Q). Resistance factors: φt=0.90/0.75, φc=0.90, φb=0.90, φv=1.00. Loads (1.2D + 1.6L). LRFD/EC3 give ~8–12% lighter sections for high live-to-dead load ratios.
Steel Grades & Strengths St 37 (Fy=240, Fu=360), St 44 (Fy=280, Fu=440), St 52 (Fy=360, Fu=520 MPa). S235, S275, S355, S420, S460 (Yield varies by thickness t ≤ 16, 40mm). A36 (Fy=250), A572 Gr 50 (Fy=345), A992 (Fy=345, Fu=450 MPa). EC3 accounts for thickness degradation; AISC and ECP use nominal thickness groups.
Compression & Buckling Cc=√(2π²E/Fy). Inelastic: Fa = [1-(λ/Cc)²/2]Fy / Ω. Elastic: 12π²E/(23λ²). λ̄ = λ/λ1. χ reduction factor using Ayrton-Perry formulation with curves a0, a, b, c, d. Inelastic: Fcr = [0.658^(Fy/Fe)]·Fy when KL/r ≤ 4.71√(E/Fy). Elastic: Fcr=0.877Fe. AISC and EC3 curve b match closely. ECP is slightly more conservative for intermediate slenderness.
Flexural LTB Capacity Empirical rT method with Cb factor. Parabolic transition to elastic buckling. Rigorous Mcr using C1, Iz, Iw, It. χLT reduction applied to plastic moment Wpl·fy. Three-zone approach: Zone 1 (Lb≤Lp), Zone 2 (Inelastic LTB), Zone 3 (Elastic LTB). AISC provides clearest cutoff points (Lp, Lr); EC3 provides continuous smooth curve.
Bolt Shear & Bearing Shear: Fv = 0.25–0.28 Fub. Bearing: Fp = α·Fu (α=0.6–1.2 based on edge distance e1). Shear: Fv,Rd = αv·fub·As/γM2 (αv=0.6). Bearing: k1·αb·fu·d·t/γM2. Shear: φ·Fnv·Ab (Fnv=370 MPa for Gr 8.8 threads excl). Bearing: 1.2lc·t·Fu ≤ 2.4dtFu. AISC uses gross shank area Ab; EC3 uses tensile stress area As. Corrected EC3 interaction formula.
Fillet Weld Design Allowable shear on throat: Fwv = 0.20 Fu (e.g., 83 MPa for E60, 96 MPa for E70). Combined: √(σ²+3τ²) ≤ 1.1(0.2Fu). Directional method: √(σ⊥²+3τ⊥²+3τ∥²) ≤ fu/(βw·γM2). Simplified: fvw,d. Directional increase: 0.60 FEXX·(1 + 0.50 sin¹·⁵θ). Up to 50% stronger transverse. AISC directional method is the most economical for transverse welds (1.5x bonus).
Beam-Column Interaction Linear / Amplified: fa/Fa + Cm·fb/((1-fa/F'e)Fb) ≤ 1.0. Also checks cross-section. Method 2 Annex B: Multi-variable kyy, kyz, kzy, kzz factors checking both axes. Bilinear H1-1: Pr/Pc + 8/9(Mrx/Mcx + Mry/Mcy) ≤ 1.0 (or Pr/(2Pc) + ... for Pr/Pc < 0.2). AISC H1-1 is by far the fastest and most designer-friendly; EC3 is the most comprehensive.

🇪🇬 ECP 205 #1 Allowable Tensile Stress on Gross Section
ECP 205
Cl. 3-5-1
ft ≤ Ft_gross
Ft_gross = Fy / Ω_y
DEMAND
ft = P / A_gross

P = Applied tensile force (kN)
A_gross = Gross cross-sectional area (mm²)
CAPACITY
Ft_gross = Fy / 1.67

Fy = Yield strength of steel (MPa)
Ω_y = 1.67  → Factor of safety against yielding (ASD)
📌 ASD basis. Use gross area without bolt holes.
🇪🇬 ECP 205 #2 Allowable Tensile Stress on Net Section
ECP 205
Cl. 3-5-2
ft_net ≤ Ft_net
Ft_net = Fu / Ω_u
DEMAND
ft_net = P / A_net

A_net = A_gross − n·d_h·t

n = no. bolt holes in critical path
d_h = bolt hole diameter (mm)
t = plate thickness (mm)
CAPACITY
Ft_net = 0.5 × Fu

Fu = Ultimate tensile strength (MPa)
Ω_u = 2.00  → Factor of safety against fracture
📌 When bolt holes present. Net area governs fracture path.
🇪🇬 ECP 205 #3 Governing Allowable Tensile Capacity
ECP 205
Cl. 3-5
P_allow = min(A_gross×Ft_gross , A_net×Ft_net)
DEMAND
P_applied = Total axial tension force (kN)
CAPACITY
P_allow = MIN(A_g×Fy/1.67 , A_net×Fu/2.0)

Final allowable = lesser of yielding and fracture
📌 Check both; governing (lesser) controls design.
🇪🇬 ECP 205 #4 Slenderness Limit (Tension Member)
ECP 205
Cl. 3-5-5
L / r_min ≤ 300
DEMAND
L = Unsupported length of member (mm)
r_min = Minimum radius of gyration (mm)

L/r = Slenderness ratio (dimensionless)
CAPACITY
300  → Maximum slenderness ratio for main tension members
(400 for secondary/bracing members per ECP)
📌 To avoid vibration and sag problems. Not a stress limit.
🇪🇺 Eurocode 3 #5 Plastic Resistance of Gross Cross-Section
EN 1993-1-1
§6.2.3(2)a
N_Ed ≤ N_pl,Rd
N_pl,Rd = A·fy / γ_M0
DEMAND
N_Ed = Design axial tensile force (kN)
  → Factored load from ULS combination
  → Always the left-side 'demand'
CAPACITY
A  = Gross cross-sectional area (mm²)
fy = Nominal yield strength (MPa) — from EN 1993-1-1 Table 3.1
γ_M0 = 1.00  → Partial factor for cross-section resistance
         (accounts for material & geometric variability)
📌 Governs when section has no holes.
Plastic redistribution allowed for Class 1–3.
🇪🇺 Eurocode 3 #6 Net Section Fracture Resistance
EN 1993-1-1
§6.2.3(2)b
N_Ed ≤ N_u,Rd
N_u,Rd = 0.9·A_net·fu / γ_M2
DEMAND
N_Ed = Design axial tensile force (kN)
CAPACITY
0.9  → Reduction coefficient accounting for stress concentrations at bolt holes
A_net = Net area after deducting holes (mm²)
         A_net = A_gross − n·d_0·t  (for non-staggered)
fu   = Ultimate tensile strength (MPa)
γ_M2 = 1.25  → Partial factor for net section fracture
         (higher than γ_M0 — less predictable failure mode)
📌 Critical when holes reduce area significantly.
0.9 factor from statistical analysis of test data.
🇪🇺 Eurocode 3 #7 Design Tension Resistance (Governing)
EN 1993-1-1
§6.2.3(2)
N_t,Rd = min(N_pl,Rd , N_u,Rd)
DEMAND
Unity ratio = N_Ed / N_t,Rd ≤ 1.0
CAPACITY
N_t,Rd = lesser of:
  (A·fy/γ_M0)  vs  (0.9·A_net·fu/γ_M2)

If both holes + yielding govern, use minimum.
📌 Design tension resistance is the smaller value.
Unity check must be ≤ 1.0 for adequacy.
🇪🇺 Eurocode 3 #8 Block Shear Resistance (EN 1993-1-8)
EN 1993-1-8
§3.10.2
N_Ed ≤ V_eff,1,Rd
V_eff,1,Rd = fu·A_nt/γ_M2 + (fy/√3)·A_nv/γ_M0
DEMAND
N_Ed = Design force causing block shear (kN)
CAPACITY
fu   = Ultimate tensile strength (MPa)
A_nt = Net area in tension perpendicular to load (mm²)
γ_M2 = 1.25  → Fracture partial factor
fy   = Yield strength (MPa)
√3   → Shear yield factor from von Mises criterion (≈ 0.577)
A_nv = Net area in shear parallel to load (mm²)
γ_M0 = 1.00  → Yielding partial factor
📌 Block shear = simultaneous tension fracture + shear yielding.
Check both failure paths.
🇪🇺 Eurocode 3 #9 Slenderness Limit (Tension)
EN 1993-1-1
§6.2.3
(NOTE: NA)
L / r_min ≤ 400
DEMAND
L = Member length between restraints (mm)
r_min = Minimum radius of gyration (mm)
CAPACITY
400  → Recommended max slenderness for tension members
(National Annex may modify)
📌 Serviceability limit — prevent excessive deformation & vibration.
🇺🇸 AISC 360-22 #10 Tensile Yielding on Gross Section (LRFD)
AISC 360-22
Ch. D2(a)
P_u ≤ φ_t · P_n(yield)
P_n = F_y · A_g
DEMAND
P_u = Required tensile strength (kN)
  → Factored load from ASCE 7 LRFD combination
  → e.g., 1.2D + 1.6L
CAPACITY
φ_t = 0.90  → Resistance factor for tensile yielding
        (reliability calibration, ASD Ω_t = 1.67)
P_n  = Nominal tensile strength (kN)
F_y  = Specified minimum yield stress (MPa)
A_g  = Gross cross-sectional area (mm²)
📌 φ_t = 0.90 corresponds to Ω_t = 1.67 in ASD.
Gross area; no hole deduction.
🇺🇸 AISC 360-22 #11 Tensile Rupture on Net Section (LRFD)
AISC 360-22
Ch. D2(b)
P_u ≤ φ_t · P_n(rupture)
P_n = F_u · A_e
DEMAND
P_u = Required tensile strength (kN)
CAPACITY
φ_t = 0.75  → Lower resistance factor for rupture
        (more brittle failure, less predictable)
F_u  = Specified min. ultimate tensile strength (MPa)
A_e  = Effective net area (mm²)
       A_e = A_net · U
U    = Shear lag factor — accounts for non-uniform
       stress distribution when not all elements connected
       (Table D3.1: U = 1 if all elements connected;
        U = 1−x̄/L for single/double angle, WT, etc.)
📌 φ_t = 0.75 for fracture (vs 0.90 for yielding).
Shear lag factor U is AISC's unique contribution.
🇺🇸 AISC 360-22 #12 ASD Tensile Yielding
AISC 360-22
Ch. D2(a)
ASD
P_a ≤ P_n / Ω_t
P_n / Ω_t = F_y·A_g / 1.67
DEMAND
P_a = Required tensile strength — ASD (kN)
  → Unfactored (service) loads
CAPACITY
Ω_t = 1.67  → ASD safety factor against yielding

🇪🇺 Eurocode 3 (EN 1993-1-8) #2 T-stub Model — Effective Length

  Mode 3: Bolt failure only

Mode 2: F_T,2,Rd = (2·M_pl,2,Rd + n·Σ(F_t,Rd)) / (m+n)
